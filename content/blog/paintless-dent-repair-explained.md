Paintless dent repair, or PDR, is a technique that removes dents by massaging the metal back into its original shape from behind the panel, using specialized rods, picks, and glue-pull tools. Because the original factory paint and clear coat are never disturbed, there's no sanding, filling, or repainting involved — which means no risk of color mismatch and a much lower price tag than traditional bodywork.

## Which Dents Qualify for PDR?

The catch is that PDR only works under specific conditions.

- The paint at the dent site needs to be intact, with no cracking or chipping.
- The metal itself needs to still have some flexibility left.
- Dents that have severely stretched the metal, or that sit right on a crease or body line, are harder or sometimes impossible to fully correct with PDR alone.

Hail damage, door dings, shopping cart dents, and most minor collision dents are excellent candidates.

## How a Mobile PDR Appointment Works

During a mobile PDR appointment, our technician will first inspect the dent with a reflection board or LED lighting to map its exact shape and depth. Depending on where the dent is located, we'll either access it from behind the panel (through wheel wells, trunk openings, or door interiors) or use a glue-pull system with tabs applied directly to the dent's surface. Working patiently from the edges inward, the metal is gradually pushed and pulled back to its original contour.

## How Much Does PDR Cost?

Most single dents — the classic shopping cart ding or small hail dent — take between 45 minutes and two hours to correct, and pricing typically runs $75 to $250 per dent depending on size, depth, and panel access. Larger dents or clusters of hail damage are usually quoted as a package rather than per-dent.

## Why PDR Is Often the Best Option

The biggest advantage of PDR beyond cost is speed and value retention: your car keeps its original factory paint, which matters for resale value and for avoiding the subtle color-match differences that can happen with a respray. It's also why insurance companies frequently prefer PDR for hail damage claims — it's faster, cheaper, and preserves originality.

## Not Sure If Your Dent Qualifies?

Send us a clear photo taken in good lighting, ideally from a couple of angles, and we can usually tell within minutes whether it's a straightforward PDR job or something that will need traditional filler-and-paint work instead.
