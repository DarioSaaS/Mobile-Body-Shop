The quarter panel — the section of body between the rear door and the trunk or bed — takes a lot of abuse from shopping carts, tight parking, and low-speed contact. Because it often includes a sharp body line or crease running through it, damage here is usually more involved to repair correctly than a simple door ding.

## Why Quarter Panel Damage Is Different From a Door Ding

A standard door ding is a shallow, rounded dent on a flat section of panel — one of the easiest things to fix with paintless dent repair. Quarter panel damage is often different for two reasons:

- **Body lines and creases.** Many quarter panels have a sharp crease or character line running through them. A dent that crosses this line is much harder to correct with PDR alone, since the line itself needs to stay perfectly straight.
- **Panel size and curvature.** Quarter panels are larger and more curved than a door, which makes color-matching and blending more noticeable if not done carefully.

## Can It Be Fixed Without Paint?

It depends on where the damage sits:

- A shallow dent on a flat section of the quarter panel, away from any crease, is often still a good PDR candidate.
- A dent or crease that crosses a body line, or that has stretched the metal, typically needs traditional bodywork: light filler, sanding, priming, and a color-matched, blended repaint.

A technician can usually tell which category your damage falls into just from a couple of clear photos.

## The Repair Process for a Crease or Dent Requiring Paint

1. Assess the damage and confirm whether PDR alone is sufficient or if paint work is needed.
2. If needed, gently reshape the metal as close to its original contour as possible before filling.
3. Apply a thin layer of filler, sand it smooth, and prime the area.
4. Color-match to your factory paint code and apply base coat and clear coat, blending several inches into the surrounding undamaged panel so there's no visible line.

Most single-panel quarter panel repairs are completed in one mobile visit, typically three to six hours depending on the extent of the crease.

## What It Costs

- **PDR-eligible dents** (no paint needed): $100–$300.
- **Creases or dents needing filler and paint blending**: $300–$700.
- **Damage extending across a body line or requiring wider blending into adjacent panels**: can run toward the higher end of that range or beyond, depending on size.

This is still typically a fraction of a full quarter panel replacement, which involves cutting, welding, or bonding in a new panel and can run well into the thousands.
