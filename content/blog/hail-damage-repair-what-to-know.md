Monsoon season in the Phoenix and Mesa area can bring fast-moving storms with surprisingly large hail, and it's not unusual for a single storm to leave dozens of small dents across a hood, roof, and trunk. If you've just discovered hail damage on your vehicle, here's a practical rundown of what to do next.

## Step 1: Document Everything

Take clear photos of every affected panel in daylight, along with a few wide shots showing the overall pattern of damage. This documentation matters if you decide to file an insurance claim, since adjusters will want to see the extent of the damage before authorizing repair.

## Step 2: Understand Why PDR Is Usually the Right Fix

Most hail dents are ideal candidates for paintless dent repair. Because hail dents rarely crack the paint, PDR can typically restore the panel to its original shape without any filler or repainting — which is exactly why most insurance carriers prefer and often specifically request PDR for hail claims. It's faster, less expensive, and preserves your factory paint.

## Step 3: Know Your Policy

Comprehensive coverage (not liability-only) is what typically covers hail damage, and most policies do cover it since hail is considered a weather event rather than an at-fault collision — meaning it generally won't affect your rates the way an at-fault accident claim would. Check your deductible amount before deciding whether filing a claim makes financial sense for smaller damage.

## Step 4: Get an Independent Assessment

Insurance adjusters sometimes undercount dents, especially small ones that are easy to miss in a quick visual inspection. A mobile PDR technician can do a thorough panel-by-panel count using reflection boards, giving you a more accurate number to reference if you need to dispute an adjuster's estimate.

## Step 5: Consider Timing

After a significant regional hailstorm, traditional shops can become backed up for weeks as claims flood in. Mobile repair technicians, working by appointment across a wider service area, are often able to get to your vehicle faster — which matters if you're trying to get repairs done before reselling, trading in, or simply want your car looking normal again.
