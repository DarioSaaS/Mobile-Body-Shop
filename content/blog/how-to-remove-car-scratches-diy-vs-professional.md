Not all scratches are created equal, and understanding which category yours falls into is the key to choosing the right fix. Automotive paint is made up of several layers: the clear coat on top, the color coat beneath it, and in some cases a primer layer below that. A scratch that only affects the clear coat can often be polished out at home. A scratch that cuts into the color coat or primer usually needs professional attention.

## The Fingernail Test: Is Your Scratch Shallow or Deep?

The classic fingernail test is a good starting point.

- Run your fingernail gently across the scratch.
- If your nail doesn't catch on the edge, it's likely a clear-coat-only scratch — a great candidate for a DIY scratch removal kit or a professional machine polish.
- If your nail catches, or you can see a different color (like gray primer or bare metal) inside the scratch, you're dealing with deeper damage that will need filler, primer, and paint to properly repair.

## When a DIY Scratch Removal Kit Works

DIY scratch removal kits, which typically include a rubbing compound and microfiber pads, can work well on light surface scratches and swirl marks. The trade-off is time, elbow grease, and inconsistent results — it's easy to apply uneven pressure and create new swirl marks or thin out the clear coat in one spot. For a single small scratch, this might be worth the afternoon. For multiple scratches across a panel, most people find the results disappointing compared to a professional pass.

## What Professional Mobile Polishing Does Differently

Professional mobile polishing uses variable-speed rotary or dual-action polishers with graduated compounds — starting aggressive to level the scratch, then stepping down to a finishing polish that restores gloss and clarity. This is the same equipment used in high-end detail shops, brought directly to your driveway. A trained technician can typically fully remove light-to-moderate clear coat scratches in 30–90 minutes per panel, with a finish that's noticeably glossier than any DIY kit can achieve.

## When Polishing Won't Be Enough

For deeper scratches that have broken through to the color coat, no amount of polishing will fix it — the area needs to be filled, primed, color-matched, and clear-coated. This is a job for a mobile paint specialist, not a compound. Attempting to polish out a deep scratch will only remove more clear coat around the damaged area and can make the eventual professional repair more expensive.
