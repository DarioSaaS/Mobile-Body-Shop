Grey scuff marks from a curb, a tight parking garage pillar, or another car's bumper are some of the most common damage we see — and also some of the easiest and cheapest to fix. If you've got scraped paint or plastic transfer on a bumper corner, here's exactly what's involved in getting it repaired.

## What Causes Bumper Scuffs and Curb Rash

Most bumper scuffs come from one of a few everyday situations: backing too close to a curb or wheel stop, brushing a parking garage column, or light contact with another vehicle's bumper in a tight lot. Unlike a crack or dent, this type of damage is usually surface-level — the plastic itself often isn't broken, but paint has been scraped, transferred, or worn away.

## Reading the Damage: Three Common Levels

Not all scuffs need the same level of repair:

- **Paint transfer only**: another surface's paint (often grey or black from a curb or tire) sits on top of your factory paint but hasn't actually scraped it off. Often correctable with compound and polishing alone.
- **Surface scraping**: the clear coat and some color coat have been worn away, leaving a dull or lightly discolored patch. Needs sanding, priming, color-matching, and clear coat.
- **Deep scraping or gouging**: the plastic bumper itself shows texture or shallow grooves. Needs filler before priming and paint.

A quick photo is usually enough for a technician to tell which category your damage falls into.

## The Mobile Repair Process

1. Clean and assess the scuffed area to confirm the damage level.
2. For paint-transfer-only cases, apply compound and polish to lift the foreign paint without disturbing your factory finish.
3. For scraped or gouged areas, sand the surface, apply flexible bumper filler if needed, and prime.
4. Color-match using your vehicle's factory paint code and apply base coat and clear coat, blending the edges into the surrounding panel.

Most bumper scuff repairs are completed in two to three hours and are ready to drive on the same visit.

## What It Costs

Pricing depends on how deep the damage goes:

- **Paint transfer / light surface scuffs**: $80–$200.
- **Deeper scraping through the clear and color coat**: $200–$350.
- **Gouging that needs filler and reshaping**: $250–$450.

Compare that to a full bumper cover replacement, which — once you factor in the part, labor, and paint — commonly runs $800 or more. For the vast majority of scuffs and curb rash, repair is both faster and dramatically cheaper.
