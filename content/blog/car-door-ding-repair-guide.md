If you've ever come back to your car in a grocery store parking lot to find a small, round dent in your door, you already know how common this type of damage is. Door dings are usually caused by another vehicle's door swinging open too close, a shopping cart rolling into the panel, or a stray golf ball or hailstone. The good news: they're also one of the most repairable types of vehicle damage.

## What Exactly Is a "Door Ding"?

A door ding is a small, shallow dent — usually one to three inches across — caused by a low-impact bump rather than a collision. Unlike scratches or scrapes, a true door ding almost never breaks the paint surface, which is exactly what makes it such a good candidate for paintless dent repair (PDR).

## Signs Your Door Ding Is a Good PDR Candidate

Before booking a repair, check for these signs:

- **The paint is unbroken** — no cracking, chipping, or bare metal visible inside the dent.
- **The dent isn't on a sharp body line or crease** — dents on flat panel surfaces respond best to PDR.
- **The metal doesn't feel overly stretched** — very deep, sharp dents sometimes need traditional filler-and-paint work instead.

If your dent checks all three boxes, there's a very good chance it can be fixed without any paint work at all.

## How Mobile Door Ding Repair Works

1. A technician inspects the dent using a reflection board to map its exact depth and shape.
2. Access is gained from behind the door panel (often through the interior door skin) or via a glue-pull system on the exterior.
3. The metal is gradually massaged back into its original contour, working from the edges inward.
4. The panel is inspected under angled light to confirm the surface is smooth and paint is undisturbed.

Most single dings are finished in under 90 minutes, with no drying time needed since no paint or filler was used.

## What It Costs

Door ding repair is one of the most affordable services in mobile auto body repair, typically running $75 to $200 per dent. Multiple dings on the same door or across several panels are usually quoted as a package, since a technician can work through them more efficiently in one visit.

## Should You Just Live With It?

A lot of drivers put off fixing a small door ding because it seems minor. Two things are worth considering: dings can trap moisture at the paint's edge over time if the finish is ever compromised, and even small cosmetic dents measurably affect trade-in and resale valuations during a used-car inspection. Since PDR is fast and affordable, most drivers find it's worth handling right away rather than letting several dings accumulate.
