If you're a few weeks out from returning a leased vehicle, it's worth taking a slow walk around your car with fresh eyes. Lease-end inspections are more thorough than most drivers expect, and the dents, scratches, and bumper scuffs you've been ignoring for two or three years are exactly what inspectors are trained to flag — and bill for.

## Why Lease Return Inspections Are Stricter Than You Think

Leasing companies typically outsource the final vehicle inspection to a third-party inspection service, not the dealership you leased from. These inspectors go panel by panel with a checklist, documenting anything outside of normal wear and tear with photos and measurements. Unlike a casual glance from a salesperson, this process is designed to catch exactly the kind of cosmetic damage that's easy to overlook during years of daily driving.

## "Normal Wear and Tear" vs. a Billable Dent

Most leasing companies use a rough sizing guide to separate acceptable wear from chargeable damage:

- **Generally acceptable**: small stone chips, light surface scratches that don't reach the base coat, and minor scuffs smaller than roughly the size of a coin.
- **Usually billable**: dents larger than a business card, any scratch that reaches the metal or primer, cracked or dented bumpers, and clusters of smaller dents on a single panel (which inspectors often treat as one larger repair item).

The gray area in between is exactly where a quick mobile inspection before you return the car pays for itself — a technician can tell you honestly whether something is likely to get flagged or not.

## What Lease-End Damage Fees Really Cost

Turn-in damage fees are billed per item, and they add up faster than most people expect:

- **Small door dings and dents**: typically $50–$150 each.
- **Larger dents or creased panels**: often $150–$300 each.
- **Scratches through the clear coat**: commonly $100–$300 depending on length and panel.
- **Cracked or scuffed bumpers**: can run $200–$500 or more if replacement is required instead of repair.

A car with a handful of small dents and a scuffed bumper can easily generate $500–$1,500 in combined charges on the final invoice — often more than the cost of getting everything repaired beforehand.

## Why Mobile PDR Is Ideal for Lease Return Prep

Getting repairs done before your inspection is almost always cheaper than paying the fees afterward, and mobile repair fits the lease-return timeline better than a traditional body shop for a few reasons:

1. **No drop-off required.** You keep driving the car right up until your return date instead of losing it to a shop for days.
2. **Faster turnaround.** Most dent and scratch repairs are completed in a few hours, right in your driveway or office parking lot.
3. **Lower cost than fees.** Paintless dent repair and factory color-matched touch-ups typically cost less than the equivalent turn-in charges, especially once you're fixing three or four items at once.
4. **One appointment, multiple panels.** A mobile technician can usually address several dents and scratches in a single visit, which is more efficient than scheduling separate shop visits.

## A Simple Pre-Return Checklist

Before your scheduled turn-in appointment, walk the car in good daylight and check:

- All four bumpers and fenders for scuffs, cracks, or curb rash
- Doors and quarter panels for dings, especially near handles and lower edges
- The hood and roof for hail dents or small creases
- Mirrors and trim for cracks or loose pieces
- Wheels for curb rash (some leasing companies bill this separately from body damage)
