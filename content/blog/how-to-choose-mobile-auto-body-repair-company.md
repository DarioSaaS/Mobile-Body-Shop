Mobile auto body repair has grown quickly because it's convenient and often cheaper than a traditional shop — but that growth also means the quality bar between providers varies a lot. Before you hand over your keys (or your driveway) to anyone, here are seven questions worth asking.

## 1. Are You Insured?

This is the single most important question. A legitimate mobile technician carries liability insurance in case something goes wrong during the repair — overspray on a neighboring panel, an accidental scratch, or equipment damage. Don't be shy about asking directly; a professional operation will have an answer ready.

## 2. Can You Show Me Examples of Completed Work?

Photos of actual past jobs — ideally before-and-after shots from real customers — tell you more than any sales pitch. Be cautious of companies that can only show stock photos or generic marketing images instead of real repairs.

## 3. What's Included in the Quote?

Ask whether the quoted price includes:

- Materials (filler, primer, paint, clear coat)
- Labor
- Any necessary color-matching
- A guarantee or warranty on the work

A vague, one-line quote with no breakdown is a red flag. A trustworthy technician will walk you through what you're paying for.

## 4. How Do You Handle Paint Matching?

Ask specifically how color matching is done — by factory paint code, by a spectrophotometer scan, or by eye. Factory code plus a physical test spray is the gold standard; "by eye" alone is a warning sign for anything beyond the smallest touch-ups.

## 5. Do You Offer Any Warranty?

Reputable mobile repair companies typically stand behind their work with at least a basic warranty against paint failure, peeling, or the dent returning. If a company won't offer any guarantee at all, treat that as a signal to keep looking.

## 6. How Long Have You Been in Business?

Mobile repair doesn't require a storefront, which unfortunately makes it easy for inexperienced operators to enter the market. Ask how long they've been doing mobile repair specifically, not just detailing or general auto work.

## 7. What Happens If I'm Not Satisfied?

A confident, established company will have a clear answer for this — whether it's a touch-up revisit, a partial refund, or another resolution path. Vague or defensive answers to this question are worth paying attention to.

## Putting It All Together

None of these questions take more than a text message or a quick phone call to answer, and a company that's confident in its work won't hesitate to answer all seven. If you get evasive answers, vague pricing, or no proof of past work, it's worth getting a second opinion before booking.
